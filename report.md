## Introduction

Inflation affects the purchasing power of households because it increases the prices of goods and services. When prices rise faster than income, people can buy fewer products with the same amount of money. In Colombia, inflation has been an important economic issue because food, housing, transportation, and other basic needs directly affect family budgets. International organizations such as the World Bank, the International Monetary Fund, and the OECD monitor inflation because it influences poverty, economic stability, and standards of living (International Monetary Fund, 2024; OECD, 2024; World Bank, 2024).

## Research Question

Does inflation significantly reduce purchasing power in Colombia?

## Null Hypothesis

H0: Inflation does not significantly reduce purchasing power in Colombia.

## Alternative Hypothesis

H1: Inflation significantly reduces purchasing power in Colombia.

## Alpha Level

The significance level selected for this study is alpha = .05. This means there is a 5% risk of rejecting the null hypothesis when the null hypothesis is actually true.

## Methodology

This report uses a hypothesis-testing framework to study whether inflation has a statistically meaningful impact on purchasing power in Colombia. Inflation can be measured using consumer price index data, while purchasing power can be represented by real income or the amount of goods and services households can buy after price changes.

For a classroom-level analysis, students may collect annual inflation rates and real wage or purchasing power indicators from reliable sources. The World Bank provides inflation and economic indicator data for Colombia, the IMF publishes country-level economic outlook data, and the OECD provides policy analysis and economic reports (International Monetary Fund, 2024; OECD, 2024; World Bank, 2024). A z-test can be used when comparing a sample statistic with a benchmark value, such as a historical average or policy target.

## Results

Using the hypothesis testing method, the researcher compares the observed inflation-related purchasing power measure with a benchmark. If the calculated p-value is less than alpha = .05, the null hypothesis is rejected. This would suggest that inflation has a statistically significant negative effect on purchasing power in Colombia.

If the p-value is greater than or equal to .05, the null hypothesis is not rejected. This would mean there is not enough statistical evidence in the sample to conclude that inflation significantly reduces purchasing power, even if inflation may still create practical economic pressure for families.

## Discussion

Inflation can reduce purchasing power because higher prices make everyday goods less affordable. This effect is especially important for households with fixed or slow-growing income. In Colombia, inflation trends are connected to food prices, exchange rates, energy costs, interest rates, and global economic conditions. The IMF explains that inflation is a major factor in macroeconomic stability, while the OECD discusses how inflation and economic policy affect households and long-term growth (International Monetary Fund, 2024; OECD, 2024). World Bank data can help students observe how Colombia's inflation rate changes over time (World Bank, 2024).

Even when a hypothesis test finds statistical significance, the conclusion should be interpreted carefully. Statistical significance does not automatically describe the size of the effect. A complete interpretation should consider both the p-value and the real-world impact on families.

## Type I Error

A Type I error would occur if the researcher rejects the null hypothesis and concludes that inflation significantly reduces purchasing power in Colombia when, in reality, it does not. In this project, using alpha = .05 means the researcher accepts a 5% risk of making this error.

## Type II Error

A Type II error would occur if the researcher fails to reject the null hypothesis and concludes that there is not enough evidence that inflation reduces purchasing power when, in reality, inflation does significantly reduce purchasing power. This could happen if the sample size is too small, the data are incomplete, or the measurement of purchasing power is not accurate.

## Conclusion

Inflation is an important economic issue because it can lower purchasing power and make basic needs more expensive. Based on the hypothesis-testing framework, the researcher should compare reliable data from Colombia with a benchmark and use the p-value to decide whether the evidence is statistically significant. If p < .05, the conclusion is that inflation significantly reduces purchasing power in Colombia. If p >= .05, the conclusion is that the sample does not provide enough statistical evidence to reject the null hypothesis.
